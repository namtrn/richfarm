## Signs of bacterial wilt

A plant may wilt suddenly while the soil remains moist, with leaves staying green or only slightly yellow at first. One branch or one side of the canopy may wilt before the whole plant declines.

## How the disease progresses

Severe plants collapse and die; roots or the lower stem may turn brown and soft. Drought wilt usually comes with dry soil and may improve after correct watering, while bacterial wilt does not recover clearly while the soil is moist. Leaves alone cannot confirm it.

## First action

Do not add water only because the plant is wilting. Isolate the suspected plant, prevent soil or runoff from reaching healthy plants, and clean tools. If several plants wilt together, keep records and a suitable sample for diagnosis; do not put diseased material into reusable compost.

Do not promise that one product will cure a plant with systemic infection. Rotation and replacement-crop advice must fit the local conditions.
