## How to recognize aphids

Aphids are small, soft-bodied insects that may be green, yellow, brown, or black. They cluster on new shoots, leaf stalks, flower buds, and leaf undersides. Winged adults may appear when a colony becomes crowded or the plant is disturbed.

## Signs aphids leave on plants

Young leaves may curl, distort, or open slowly, while shoots stay short and weak. Leaves can feel sticky from honeydew and may later develop a dark sooty coating. Many ants around new growth are a reason to inspect, but they do not confirm aphids by themselves.

## First checks and safe action

Turn over leaves and inspect new shoots while watering. For a light infestation, wash the insects away with a gentle stream of water or wipe off the clusters, then prune badly crowded tips. Check new growth again over the next few days. Avoid excessive nitrogen because soft new growth is easier for aphids to colonize.

Do not treat only because leaves are curled; drought, cool weather, or a virus can look similar. On edible crops, use only a product labeled for the crop and follow its harvest interval.
