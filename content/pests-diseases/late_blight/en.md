## Signs of late blight

Late blight can produce irregular blue-gray or dark brown marks on leaves and stalks. In humid conditions, a thin growth may appear at the edge of a lesion; fruit can develop firm brown areas that later soften.

## How the disease progresses

Spread can be rapid during cool, humid weather with rain or long dew periods. Large sections of foliage may die in a short time, followed by stem and fruit damage. Nutrient stress usually develops more slowly and does not create a fast-moving water-soaked pattern.

## First action

Separate suspected plants and do not carry wet leaves or soil to another area. Remove diseased tissue in dry weather, place it in closed waste, and clean tools. Avoid overhead watering. If several plants are affected or lesions expand daily, confirm the agent before choosing a product.

The name “late blight” does not mean every rotting fruit has the same cause.
