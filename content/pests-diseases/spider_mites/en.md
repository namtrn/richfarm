## How to recognize spider mites

Spider mites are tiny and are often easiest to see with a hand lens. They live mainly on leaf undersides, where fine webbing and moving specks may appear as the population grows.

## Signs on plants

Leaves develop many small yellow or silvery stipples, then may look dull or bronze and drop early. Fine webbing can run between the leaf and its stalk or cover the underside. Hot, dry, dusty conditions increase risk, but stippling can also result from nutrient stress or sun injury.

## First checks and safe action

Hold white paper under a leaf and tap it gently; moving specks on the paper are a reason to inspect with a lens. Rinse both leaf surfaces, remove badly damaged leaves, and reduce dust around the plant. Watch new leaves over the next few days. Do not water the soil more just because leaves are stippled; adjust air humidity and drainage to the plant's actual needs.

If webbing is widespread or the plant declines quickly, identify the mite group before using a control product. Follow the label and harvest interval on edible crops.
