## Signs of powdery mildew

Powdery mildew forms white, powder-like patches on leaves, stalks, young shoots, and sometimes flower buds. It may begin as separate spots and join together as the disease develops.

## How the disease progresses

Affected leaves can yellow, distort, or dry early, while heavily infected shoots and buds grow poorly. Foliage can develop this disease without standing water on the surface, so dry leaves do not rule it out.

## Distinguishing and first action

Powdery growth usually forms patches and returns after a gentle wipe, unlike loose soil dust. Sun injury, spray residue, and spider-mite damage can also make leaves look pale. Isolate heavily affected plants, remove diseased tissue, clear fallen leaves, and open the canopy.

Do not use one product for every crop. If the disease is spreading, identify the crop and likely agent before treatment, then follow the label and harvest interval.
