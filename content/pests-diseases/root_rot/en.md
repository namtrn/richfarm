## Signs of root rot

Root rot describes a group of conditions, not one causal agent. A plant may yellow or wilt while the soil is still wet; the base can soften, roots may turn brown or black, and few white new roots remain. Waterlogged media may smell sour or rotten.

## Distinguishing the cause

Water-stress wilting usually comes with dry soil and may improve after correct watering. Waterlogging, a pathogen, and concentrated fertilizer injury can all cause yellow leaves, so inspect roots and drainage before deciding.

## First action

Pause watering, move the plant to an airy place, and check moisture around the roots. Cut away soft roots with clean tools, and repot only when this can be done without spreading contaminated media. Correct drainage and adjust watering to the plant's needs.

Do not assume one product will cure every case. If several plants wilt together, record the soil, water, and root conditions so the cause can be confirmed.
