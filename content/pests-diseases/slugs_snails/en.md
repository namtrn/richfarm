## How to recognize slugs and snails

Snails have shells and slugs do not; both hide under fallen leaves, containers, boards, or other cover close to the soil. They are easier to find at night, early morning, or after rain.

## Signs on plants

Leaves may have large irregular holes, ragged edges, or only the veins left behind. A shiny slime trail on leaves, soil, or the side of a pot is useful evidence. Caterpillars also make holes, but usually leave frass and do not leave slime trails.

## First checks and safe action

Inspect around the base, under leaves, beneath pots, and under objects on the ground while the animals are active. Pick them off when numbers are low, remove hiding places, and keep the growing area open. Protect young plants with a suitable barrier if needed, but check inside the barrier because slugs or snails may already be there.

On leafy vegetables, do not use bait or a pesticide until you have read the crop label, harvest interval, and pet warnings. The name “snails” can cover several species, so do not assign it to one scientific species without confirmation.
