## How to recognize mealybugs

Mealybugs are small insects covered in a white, cotton-like wax. They commonly sit in leaf axils, stem joints, leaf stalks, undersides, or around the roots of potted plants.

## Signs on plants

Feeding sites may turn yellow, soft, and slow-growing. Honeydew makes foliage sticky and may attract ants or a dark sooty coating. Root mealybugs can cause wilting or yellowing while the potting mix is still moist, so inspect the roots instead of simply watering more.

## First checks and safe action

Move a suspected plant away from others. Wipe small clusters with a cotton swab or damp cloth, prune heavily infested parts, and recheck closed leaf axils after a few days. Inspect new plants before placing them with the rest, and address ants that are tending the colony.

Do not scrub roots harshly or apply a product without a label for the crop. On edible plants, confirm the product and harvest interval before picking.
