## How to recognize caterpillars

“Caterpillars” describes larvae of many moths and butterflies, not one species. Larvae may be green, brown, or striped, and often hide under leaves, inside dense growth, or near fruit.

## Signs caterpillars leave on plants

Feeding creates ragged edges, from small holes to large missing sections of leaf. Dark frass may collect on leaves, in leaf axils, or beside bored fruit. Slugs and snails can also make holes, so check for slime trails and the time of damage before deciding.

## First checks and safe action

Inspect leaf undersides, leaf axils, and fruit in the cool part of the day. Pick off eggs and larvae when numbers are low, and remove badly damaged tissue from the growing area. Clear crop residue and use an appropriate mesh cover to reduce egg-laying adults.

Do not use one product name for every caterpillar. If treatment is necessary, identify the crop and larval stage first, then follow the product label and harvest interval.
