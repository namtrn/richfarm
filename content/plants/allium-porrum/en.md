Leek is a cold-hardy allium prized for its sweet, layered white stem and sturdy, fan-shaped flat leaves. It has a lengthy growing cycle, prefers cool temperatures, and endures light frosts with resilience. The defining aspect of leek culture is progressive soil hilling, or blanching, which shields the buried shaft from sunlight to maximize the tender, succulent white portion.

## Light and location

Leeks demand at least 6 hours of full sunshine daily to build robust stalk girth and rich flavor. Optimal growth occurs in cool conditions between 15-22°C.

Plant in open garden trenches or deep raised planters that receive unobstructed sun and steady airflow.

## Watering

Consistent deep hydration is essential across the long development period. Water deeply around the root zone, preventing moisture deficits that turn leek shanks tough, fibrous, and bitter.

Water thoroughly after each hilling cycle, ensuring water percolates cleanly without waterlogging the root base.

## Soil and potting mix

Deep, stone-free, friable soil rich in decomposed organic matter and with a pH of 6.5-7.0 is vital. Loosening the soil thoroughly encourages straight, unhindered downward shaft growth.

For container culture, choose deep planters or troughs with at least 30-40 cm depth to accommodate progressive soil building.

## Fertilizer

As prolonged growers, leeks require sustained fertility. Incorporate ample mature compost or composted manure into the trench base before planting.

Side-dress with balanced organic nutrients every 3-4 weeks in conjunction with weeding and stem-hilling tasks.

## Planting and propagation

Start seeds indoors and grow until seedlings achieve pencil thickness (about 15 cm tall). Trim long roots and upper foliage slightly, then drop individual seedlings into dibbled holes 10-15 cm deep.

Fill each hole with water rather than compacted soil; the soil naturally washes in around the stem as the seedling establishes.

## Care and harvesting

Periodic blanching is essential: mound soil, straw, or collars gradually up the stems as they grow, keeping the lower shanks shaded and white. Keep beds meticulously weeded.

Harvest 90-120 days after transplanting when shanks reach 2-3 cm in diameter. Ease plants upward with a garden fork rather than pulling directly by hand to prevent stalk breakage.

## Common pests and diseases

Look out for [thrips](richfarm://pests-diseases/thrips) tucked within inner leaf folds. Occasional leaf-chewing [caterpillars](richfarm://pests-diseases/caterpillars) can damage outer foliage.

Persistent dampness can trigger foliar [rust](richfarm://pests-diseases/rust) pustules or underground [root rot](richfarm://pests-diseases/root_rot). Ensure ample plant spacing to maintain drying airflow through the leaf canopy.
