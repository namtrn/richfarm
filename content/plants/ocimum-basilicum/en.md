Basil grows quickly in warm weather, but leaf quality drops when the plant is stressed by poor drainage, repeated drying, or unchecked flowering. The most useful routine is simple: give it strong light, water according to the soil rather than a fixed schedule, and harvest by cutting growing tips so the plant keeps branching.

## Give it strong light and watch heat stress

Grow basil in a bright position with plenty of direct sun. In very hot weather, especially in containers, leaves may droop during the hottest part of the day even when the soil is still moist. Check the soil before adding more water; if it remains damp, temporary afternoon shade may be more useful than another soaking.

Crowded plants stay wet longer after rain and are harder to inspect. Leave enough room for air to move through the foliage, particularly in humid conditions.

## Water when the surface begins to dry

Basil prefers evenly moist soil with good drainage. Water thoroughly around the root zone when the surface begins to dry, then allow excess water to drain. Container plants may need checking more often than plants in garden soil because pots warm and dry quickly.

Do not leave a pot standing in collected runoff. Persistently wet soil can damage roots and may cause wilting even though the pot still feels wet. When practical, water at soil level so the foliage does not remain wet for long periods.

## Pinch and harvest to keep new shoots coming

Once the plant has several healthy sets of leaves, harvest or pinch a growing tip just above a leaf node. New shoots can then develop from the buds below the cut, making the plant bushier over time.

Repeat this as stems become long enough to harvest rather than following a rigid calendar. Cutting whole shoot tips is usually more useful than stripping many individual leaves from the lower stem.

Flowering changes the plant's growth pattern. If your goal is a continued leaf harvest, remove developing flower spikes when you notice them and keep harvesting young shoots. If you want flowers or seed, allow selected stems to bloom instead.

## Feed lightly and propagate when useful

Basil does not need heavy feeding when it is growing in fertile soil enriched with compost. Container plants or repeatedly harvested plants may benefit from a light fertilizer application according to the product label. Avoid trying to force soft, rapid growth with excessive fertilizer.

New plants can be started from seed. Healthy non-flowering shoots can also be rooted as cuttings in water or moist potting mix. Replace an aging plant when stems become woody and new leaf production noticeably slows rather than at a fixed age.

## Check pests and diseases early

### Pests

[Aphids](richfarm://pests-diseases/aphids) often gather on tender tips and leaf undersides. Curled new growth, sticky residue, or clusters of small insects are useful signs. Small colonies can often be rinsed away or removed by hand.

[Slugs and snails](richfarm://pests-diseases/slugs_snails) chew irregular holes in tender leaves, especially in damp, sheltered areas. Check around pots and beneath nearby debris in the evening or early morning.

### Diseases

[Downy mildew](richfarm://pests-diseases/downy_mildew) can cause yellowing between leaf veins with darker growth on the underside in humid conditions. Remove badly affected leaves, improve airflow, and avoid keeping the foliage wet.

[Root rot](richfarm://pests-diseases/root_rot) is more likely when the root zone stays saturated. If a plant wilts while the soil remains wet, inspect drainage and roots before adding more water.

[Leaf spot](richfarm://pests-diseases/leaf_spot) may appear as expanding dark lesions on foliage. Remove heavily affected leaves and reduce repeated splashing from soil onto the plant. Because basil is eaten fresh, identify the problem before using any treatment and follow the product label and harvest interval where applicable.
