## Light and location

Thyme is a low shrub that grows best in full sun with well-drained soil. It needs at least 6 hours of sun a day for fragrant leaves and a compact shape; too little light makes it stretch, thin out, and lose aroma.

This is a small woody plant that spreads into a low mat close to the ground and does not climb, so it needs no support. It suits edging beds, growing between paving stones, or pots; the plant spreads wider over the years while staying low. In a container, choose a wide, shallow pot, since the roots spread sideways.

## Watering

Thyme tolerates drought well and cannot handle soil that holds water for long. Water when the top layer of soil has dried completely; let the soil dry fully between waterings. Overwatering or poor drainage rots the roots and kills the plant, especially in pots or in the rainy season.

Once established, its water needs drop noticeably. In containers, check the moisture by hand before watering; in winter or cool, damp weather, water very little.

## Soil

Choose well-drained, somewhat dry soil, such as sandy soil or mix amended with grit or perlite. Thyme does better in lean soil than in very rich soil. Avoid heavy soil that holds water; use a raised bed or container if the garden soil drains poorly.

## Feeding

Thyme needs almost no fertilizer; overly rich soil gives soft, less aromatic growth that tolerates drought poorly. If the soil is very poor, a small amount of well-rotted compost worked in at planting is enough. Avoid feeding while the plant is growing normally.

## Sowing and propagation

Thyme can be grown from seed, cuttings, or division. Seed germinates slowly; sow it shallowly in well-draining soil and keep it lightly moist. Cuttings are faster: take a healthy young shoot, place it in well-draining medium, and keep it lightly moist until roots form. Division is simplest once the plant has spread into a mat.

Plant at the start of the growing season so the roots establish in time. Young plants need more regular watering for the first few weeks, tapering off once they are rooted.

## Ongoing care and harvest

Light, regular trimming keeps the plant compact and produces fresh growth evenly. After flowering, trim to restore the shape; avoid cutting deep into old wood, which sprouts poorly. A spreading plant can be left as a ground cover or trimmed back as you prefer.

Pick leaves and young stems whenever you need them. Cut young stems and strip the leaves, or use whole small sprigs; the leaves are most aromatic when picked in the morning before the sun is strong. To dry them, hang the stems in an airy place out of direct sun.

## Common pests and diseases

[Spider mites](richfarm://pests-diseases/spider_mites) can appear in hot, dry weather, pale-stippling the leaves and leaving fine webbing; check the leaf undersides and keep the plant healthy. [Aphids](richfarm://pests-diseases/aphids) may gather on young shoots; a light infestation can be washed off or removed by hand.

In prolonged damp conditions, [root rot](richfarm://pests-diseases/root_rot) from water-retentive soil can wilt and kill the plant; stop watering and check the drainage first, and repot into a better-draining mix if it is in a container. [Powdery mildew](richfarm://pests-diseases/powdery_mildew) can appear when the canopy is dense and damp; thin the plant and keep it open.
