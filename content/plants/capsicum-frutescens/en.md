Bird's eye chili is a compact perennial shrub known for its small, intensely pungent upright peppers. It thrives in warm, sunny conditions and yields fruit reliably when given good drainage and steady light. The top care priority is preventing soggy soil while ensuring consistent direct sunshine for continuous blossom and fruit set.

## Light and location

Bird's eye chili requires at least 6-8 hours of direct sunlight each day to maintain sturdy branch growth and productive flowering. Insufficient sun leads to leggy stems, sparse foliage, and early flower drop.

Choose an airy balcony, patio, or garden spot that receives abundant morning sunlight. During severe midday heatwaves, light afternoon shade helps keep the plant from wilting unnecessarily.

## Watering

Keep the soil moderately moist but never waterlogged. Water thoroughly only when the top 2-3 cm of soil feels dry to the touch, and water early in the morning so excess moisture evaporates before evening.

Maintain even moisture while plants are blossoming and setting fruit, because severe dry spells trigger blossom drop. Conversely, chronic overwatering deprives roots of oxygen, leading to leaf drop and stem rot.

## Soil and potting mix

Plants require loose, humus-rich soil with rapid drainage and a target pH of 6.0-6.8. For containers, blend potting soil with compost, perlite, and coarse bark or coco coir to preserve aeration.

Select pots with at least 25-30 cm diameter and ample drainage holes. Check regularly to ensure the drain holes do not become clogged with compacted potting mix or root mats.

## Fertilizer

Incorporate mature compost or balanced organic fertilizer into the planting bed before setting out seedlings. Once side branches form and flowers emerge, transition to feeds with higher phosphorus and potassium to strengthen fruit stems.

Avoid excessive nitrogen fertilizer on mature plants, as excess nitrogen promotes lush foliage at the expense of fruit production and pungency.

## Planting and propagation

Start seeds indoors or in seedling trays with sterile seed-starting mix. Seeds germinate within 7-14 days in warm temperatures; transplant to individual containers when seedlings produce 4-5 true leaves.

Pinch out the central growing tip when the plant reaches 15-20 cm in height to encourage lateral branching and a fuller, bushier canopy.

## Care and harvesting

Prune lower foliage that touches the soil surface to maintain air circulation and stop soil-borne pathogens from splashing upward. Support main branches with small bamboo stakes if the bush carries a heavy crop.

Harvest peppers when they turn fully vibrant red or deep orange. Clip the stems with clean shears rather than pulling by hand to avoid damaging adjacent flower buds.

## Common pests and diseases

Watch for [aphids](richfarm://pests-diseases/aphids) and [thrips](richfarm://pests-diseases/thrips) congregating on tender new shoots and causing crinkled foliage. In hot, dry weather, [spider mites](richfarm://pests-diseases/spider_mites) may colonize the undersides of leaves, producing fine yellow speckling.

During sustained damp weather, poor drainage can trigger [root rot](richfarm://pests-diseases/root_rot) or [bacterial wilt](richfarm://pests-diseases/bacterial_wilt). Maintain clean mulch and immediately discard diseased foliage.
