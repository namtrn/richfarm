Potato is a cool-season tuber crop prized for its high culinary value and subterranean harvests. It requires loose, well-draining soil and suffers if daytime temperatures soar or if water stagnates around developing tubers. The essential care rule is periodic hilling to shield tubers from sunlight and keep moisture even throughout the bulking cycle.

## Light and location

Potato vines demand 6-8 hours of direct sunshine daily to fuel vigorous photosynthesis and tuber enlargement. Ideal soil temperatures for tuberization range between 15-20°C; sustained hot spells inhibit underground development.

Select open garden beds or deep growing containers with plenty of unobstructed sun exposure and good air movement.

## Watering

Maintain consistent, moderate moisture, especially from blossoming through active tuber bulking. Fluctuating between extreme drought and heavy soaking leads to cracked, misshapen, or hollow-heart tubers.

Taper off watering as the foliage naturally yellows and declines toward maturity, allowing tuber skins to cure and reducing late-season rot risks.

## Soil and potting mix

Tubers require light, well-aerated, stone-free soil with exceptional drainage and a slightly acidic pH between 5.0-6.0 to minimize scab issues. Rich sandy loam amended with well-rotted compost provides ideal growing conditions.

When utilizing grow bags or deep crates, ensure a minimum container depth of 35-45 cm to accommodate incremental soil hilling.

## Fertilizer

Incorporate generous amounts of compost or well-cured manure prior to planting. Side-dress with balanced organic nutrients when first hilling, focusing on potassium inputs once tuber development begins.

Limit heavy nitrogen applications; excessive nitrogen stimulates excessive leafy vines while delaying tuber formation and increasing disease vulnerability.

## Planting and propagation

Propagate using certified, pre-sprouted seed potatoes. Cut larger seed tubers into chunks containing at least 1-2 robust eyes and allow the cut edges to callous for 1-2 days before planting 7-10 cm deep.

As shoots reach 15 cm tall, hill soil or straw around the stems, leaving only top leaves exposed. Repeat several times as stems elongate.

## Care and harvesting

Hilling is mandatory because tubers exposed to sunlight turn green and produce toxic solanine alkaloids. Keep underground tubers covered at all times.

Harvest when the foliage naturally turns yellow and dies back completely. Carefully dig around the root perimeter on a dry day, avoiding spade cuts on the delicate skin; cure in a dark, airy place for several days before storage.

## Common pests and diseases

Sap-sucking [aphids](richfarm://pests-diseases/aphids) and [thrips](richfarm://pests-diseases/thrips) can spread foliar viruses. In cool, persistently damp weather, [late blight](richfarm://pests-diseases/late_blight) and [early blight](richfarm://pests-diseases/early_blight) can rapidly destroy leaves and rot tubers.

Excessively wet soil induces [root rot](richfarm://pests-diseases/root_rot) and soft rot in underground tubers. Rotate growing sites annually and avoid planting where nightshades grew the previous season.
