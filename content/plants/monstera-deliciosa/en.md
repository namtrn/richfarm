## Light and location

Monstera grows best in bright indirect light, such as near a curtained window. Enough light produces large leaves with the characteristic holes and splits; in too-dark corners the leaves stay small, un-split, and the stems stretch. Harsh direct sun burns the leaves, leaving yellow or brown patches.

This is a climbing plant with aerial roots, usually grown with a moss pole or support that the roots grip as the plant stands upright. As it grows, guide the aerial roots into a moist moss pole so the plant climbs naturally. Choose a stable spot, since the plant dislikes sudden changes in light and drafts.

## Watering

Water when the top layer of soil has dried, checking a few centimeters deep with your finger. The plant tolerates light dryness between waterings but not long waterlogging; overwatering is a common cause of yellow leaves and root rot. In the cold season or in low light, water less often.

When you water, soak the pot until water runs from the drainage holes, then empty any standing water from the saucer. The large leaves collect dust; wipe them regularly with a damp cloth to keep the plant photosynthesizing and to reduce pests hiding underneath.

## Soil

Use a loose, well-draining mix rather than heavy soil. A blend of houseplant mix, coco coir, pine bark, and perlite lets the roots breathe and prevents waterlogging, matching the plant's aerial-root habit. The pot needs clear drainage holes and should never be left standing in water.

## Feeding

The plant needs little fertilizer. Apply a diluted liquid fertilizer as directed every few weeks during the growing season, and reduce or stop in the cold season. Pale leaves or fewer splits are usually a sign of too little light rather than too little fertilizer; check the position before increasing the dose. Do not feed while the soil is dry.

## Sowing and propagation

Propagation is easy from stem cuttings: take a healthy piece of stem with at least one node and one leaf, cut just below the node, and place it in water or moist mix. Once the roots are a few centimeters long, pot the cutting into fresh mix. Several cuttings in one pot fill it out faster.

On older plants with long aerial roots, leave them in place or trim them when needed. When the plant outgrows its pot, move it to a larger container with fresh mix.

## Ongoing care and harvest

Trimming keeps the plant compact and encourages new leaves. When a stem grows too long, cut it back just above a node; the cut piece can be rooted as a new plant. Guide the aerial roots into the moist moss pole so the plant climbs and stands firm; a hard, dry pole gives the roots nothing to grip.

Yellow leaves usually come from overwatering or too little light; check the soil and position before acting. A few old lower leaves yellowing and falling is natural as the plant ages. Wipe the leaves regularly and rotate the pot so the plant grows evenly on all sides.

## Common pests and diseases

[Mealybugs](richfarm://pests-diseases/mealybugs) cluster in the leaf axils and along the stems as white cottony spots; wipe them off with a cotton swab or damp cloth when you first notice them. [Spider mites](richfarm://pests-diseases/spider_mites) appear when the air is dry, yellowing the leaves and leaving fine webbing underneath; raising the humidity and wiping the leaves helps keep them in check.

Overwatering causes [root rot](richfarm://pests-diseases/root_rot), shown by yellow leaves, a soft stem base, and a musty smell from the mix. If this happens, stop watering, inspect the roots, and repot into a better-draining mix. If the leaves develop spreading spots, this may be [leaf spot](richfarm://pests-diseases/leaf_spot); remove the affected leaves and avoid wetting the foliage when watering.
