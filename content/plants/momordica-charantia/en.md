Bitter melon is a vigorous tropical climbing vine grown for its distinctively bitter, oblong ridged fruit and rich culinary traditions. It excels in hot, sunny conditions and produces a continuous succession of flowers and fruit. Key management steps include providing sturdy vertical trellising, consistent hydration, and protecting young fruits from fruit-fly damage.

## Light and location

Bitter melon demands 6-8 hours of unfiltered sunshine daily to power vigorous vine extension and prolific female blossom production. Plants languish in dim or partially shaded settings.

Erect trellises in open, sunny garden quadrants, against sun-drenched boundary walls, or across airy rooftop decks.

## Watering

Large, lobed leaves transpire moisture rapidly during peak summer heat, necessitating reliable daily watering. Water deeply at ground level in the early morning and verify soil moisture again on scorching afternoons.

Avoid water stagnation; prolonged saturation suffocates root crowns, leading to yellowing foliage and blossom abortion.

## Soil and potting mix

Grow in fertile, organically rich, loamy soil with prompt drainage and a pH range of 6.0-6.8. Amending planting beds with well-decomposed manure and compost fosters a deep, responsive root system.

For container culture, utilize pots holding at least 20-30 liters with unimpeded drainage holes.

## Fertilizer

Incorporate mature compost into the planting hole before transplanting. As lateral runners extend along the trellis, apply balanced organic feedings every 2-3 weeks.

Once fruits begin sizing up, supplement with potassium and phosphorus to maintain fruit density and prevent early yellowing.

## Planting and propagation

Sow scarified seeds that have been soaked in warm water for 4-6 hours to soften the thick seed coat. Seeds emerge reliably within 3-5 days in warm soil.

Provide a sturdy 1.8-2 m trellis when vines reach 20-30 cm; pinching the primary terminal shoot at trellis height stimulates productive secondary fruiting branches.

## Care and harvesting

Guide tendrils evenly across the netting to prevent tangled foliage clumps. Prune away aged lower foliage touching the soil to maximize airflow and curb disease build-up.

Harvest fruits while still firm and crisp green, approximately 12-15 days following pollination, before they turn orange and soft. Regular picking promotes continuous blossom setting.

## Common pests and diseases

Watch for [aphids](richfarm://pests-diseases/aphids) and [thrips](richfarm://pests-diseases/thrips) on growing tips. Fruit flies frequently oviposit in young fruits, causing premature yellowing and internal rot; shield nascent fruit with breathable mesh sleeves.

In persistently humid weather, foliage can succumb to [powdery mildew](richfarm://pests-diseases/powdery_mildew) and [downy mildew](richfarm://pests-diseases/downy_mildew). Heavy waterlogged soils risk causing [root rot](richfarm://pests-diseases/root_rot).
