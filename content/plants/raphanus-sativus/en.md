White radish (daikon) is a fast-growing, cool-season root crop renowned for its crisp, succulent white taproots and sweet, mild flavor. It demands unhindered soil depth and steady, uniform moisture to prevent pithiness, splitting, and bitter pungency. The golden rules of care are sowing in loose stone-free soil, avoiding drought stress, and pulling roots punctually at peak maturity.

## Light and location

White radishes flourish with 5-6 hours of daily sunshine to energize leafy tops and power steady root bulking. Best quality is achieved under cool growing temperatures between 16-22°C.

Choose high garden beds or deep planters situated in sunny outdoor spaces with good air circulation.

## Watering

Uniform moisture is the single most critical factor in root texture. Water consistently each morning; abrupt cycles of drought followed by heavy irrigation inevitably crack root walls and spike peppery heat.

Prevent saturated waterlogging, which quickly induces tip decay and suffocation rot in the root zone.

## Soil and potting mix

Roots require deep, friable, stone-free sandy loam with an unobstructed root zone of at least 25-30 cm and a pH of 6.0-7.0. Hardpan or obstacles cause forked, stunted, or twisted taproots.

For containers, mix compost, coco coir, and perlite into potting soil to ensure unhindered root expansion.

## Fertilizer

Incorporate rich compost or worm castings thoroughly before sowing. Because white radish matures rapidly, additional heavy fertilizing is rarely required.

Avoid excess nitrogen feeds, which fuel immense foliar canopies at the expense of root density and size.

## Planting and propagation

Always direct-sow seeds, as root crops suffer irreversible damage from transplanting. Sow seeds 1-1.5 cm deep; emergence follows swiftly in 3-5 days under moist conditions.

Thin seedlings promptly when 2-3 true leaves appear, leaving 10-15 cm spacing between plants to permit proper root expansion.

## Care and harvesting

Lightly hill loose soil around exposed root shoulders to shield them from sun-induced greening. Keep beds free from weed competition with shallow hoeing.

Harvest in 45-60 days when roots reach mature size with smooth, firm shoulders. Do not leave roots in the ground past maturity, as they rapidly become hollow, woody, and pithy.

## Common pests and diseases

Watch for [aphids](richfarm://pests-diseases/aphids) and [thrips](richfarm://pests-diseases/thrips) on young foliage. Leaf-chewing [caterpillars](richfarm://pests-diseases/caterpillars) can strip green leaf blades if left unchecked.

Overly wet soils foster fungal [leaf spot](richfarm://pests-diseases/leaf_spot) and bacterial [root rot](richfarm://pests-diseases/root_rot). Ensure loose soil texture and efficient runoff paths.
