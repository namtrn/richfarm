## Light and location

Garlic is a sun-loving plant that needs full sun to form firm bulbs. It grows best where it gets direct sun for most of the day; too much shade gives leaves but small bulbs. Garlic needs a period of cool, even cold, weather early on to set its bulbs, so planting time depends strongly on the climate of each region.

This is a low plant with leaves growing in a clump from the base, so it needs no support. Container growing works if the pot is deep enough and placed in full sun; the plants need even spacing so the bulbs can develop.

## Watering

Keep the soil evenly moist while the leaves develop and the bulb begins to swell. Water when the top layer begins to dry; long dry spells give small bulbs, while soil kept soggy invites bulb rot. As the leaves begin to yellow near the end of the season, taper off watering so the bulbs dry and store well.

In containers, the soil dries faster than in garden beds and needs checking more often. Avoid wetting the leaves heavily to limit fungal disease.

## Soil

Choose loose, fertile soil with good drainage. Growing on a raised bed helps the bulbs develop evenly and avoids waterlogging in the rainy season. Work well-rotted compost into the soil before planting; avoid fresh manure, which encourages bulb rot.

## Feeding

Garlic needs steady nutrition while the leaves grow. Prepare the bed before planting, then feed lightly once the plants are established. As the bulb begins to swell, cut back on nitrogen and favor potassium for firm bulbs that store well.

Watch the leaves before adding more. Pale leaves and slow growth may signal a nutrient shortage. Keep concentrated fertilizer away from the stem and do not feed while the soil is dry.

## Sowing and propagation

Garlic is usually grown from cloves separated from a seed bulb, not from true seed. Choose a healthy bulb, separate the large outer cloves for planting, since bigger cloves give bigger bulbs, and leave the papery skin on each clove. Plant the cloves pointed end up, buried a few centimeters deep, and cover with soil.

Planting time varies by region: in areas with a real winter, plant in fall so the crop passes through the cold period; in warm climates, choose a suitable variety and plant during the cool part of the year. Learn which varieties suit your area, since not every garlic forms bulbs well everywhere.

## Ongoing care and harvest

Weed around the plants regularly, since garlic competes poorly with weeds. Keep the soil loose around the base so the bulbs develop evenly. For varieties that produce scapes, snap them off as soon as they curl so the plant channels energy into the bulb; the scapes can be eaten.

When the leaves begin to yellow and dry from the tips, the bulbs are nearly mature. Stop watering and let the bulbs dry in the soil for a few days if the weather is dry, then lift the whole plant and cure the bulbs in an airy spot until the skins dry. Well-cured bulbs store for a long time. Harvest in time before heavy rain rots the bulbs.

## Common pests and diseases

[Thrips](richfarm://pests-diseases/thrips) can leave silvery or whitish streaks on garlic leaves and slow growth. Check the leaf axils while watering; keeping the plant healthy and well watered helps it cope. [Aphids](richfarm://pests-diseases/aphids) may also gather on young leaves.

In damp conditions, [rust](richfarm://pests-diseases/rust) can form rust-colored spots on the leaves; remove affected leaves, keep the planting open, and avoid wetting the foliage. [Leaf spot](richfarm://pests-diseases/leaf_spot) can also appear when leaves stay wet. If the bulbs turn soft and rot, check the drainage and reduce watering; identify the exact cause before using any product.
