Bok choy grows quickly in mild weather, but prolonged heat can make it bolt before the juicy stalks fill out. Its shallow roots also react quickly to dry soil or standing water. Choose the right season, keep moisture even, and inspect the leaf undersides so the harvest stays crisp and clean.

## Choose a cool season and gentle light

Bok choy prefers morning sun and mild weather. In a temperate part of the United States, sow it in early spring or late summer so the crop grows before severe heat or cold. In Vietnam, the cooler, drier season is usually easier; during hot weather, give it light afternoon shade and enough space for air to move through the leaves.

This is a low plant with stalks gathered at the base, so it needs no stakes or trellis. If it stretches upward and forms a flower stalk, pick it early rather than waiting for larger, tougher stalks.

## Water according to the soil surface

Shallow roots need evenly moist soil so the stalks do not become stringy. Feel the top layer before watering. When it starts to dry, water slowly around the base so moisture reaches the roots, then let excess water drain before watering again. Reduce watering after rain.

Do not leave water pooled in the stalks or around the crown. Water at the base in the morning and use a thin mulch layer, leaving a gap around the stem so the crown can breathe.

## Use rich soil and feed modestly

Mix mature compost or well-rotted organic matter into the bed before sowing. The growing mix should hold moisture but drain quickly; containers need clear drainage holes. If leaves are pale and growth slows even though the soil is moist, spread a thin layer of compost between rows or apply a diluted liquid fertilizer according to its label.

Keep concentrated fertilizer away from the base and never feed dry soil. Excess nitrogen makes soft, crowded foliage and may attract aphids. Watch the leaf color and stalk firmness before adding more.

## Stagger sowing so plants do not age together

Small seed can be sown directly into moist soil or started in trays and transplanted after several true leaves appear. Sow shallowly, cover lightly, and keep the surface moist to the touch but not soggy. If the tray holds standing water or seedlings pinch at the stem and collapse, reduce watering and use clean medium to limit [damping-off](richfarm://pests-diseases/damping_off).

Sow small batches at intervals so young plants are ready when an earlier batch bolts. When transplanting, keep the root ball intact and water gently. Thin weak seedlings so the remaining plants have room to form full stalks.

## Harvest young stalks before bolting

Bok choy can be harvested as a whole plant once the stalks are full, or outer stalks can be removed while the center continues growing. Use a clean knife or scissors at the base and avoid bruising the stalks. If a flower stalk begins to rise, harvest the remaining young stalks early because older tissue becomes tougher.

Remove old leaves and weeds after each harvest. Open space around the crown helps foliage dry after rain and makes pests easier to see.

## Check early for pests and disease

[Caterpillars](richfarm://pests-diseases/caterpillars) chew large holes or remove sections of leaf. [Aphids](richfarm://pests-diseases/aphids) hide on young leaves and undersides of stalks, curling or coating them. [Slugs and snails](richfarm://pests-diseases/slugs_snails) make ragged holes at night in damp beds. Inspect the undersides in the morning, hand-pick a small number, and clear fallen leaves around the base.

[Downy mildew](richfarm://pests-diseases/downy_mildew) may create yellow patches above the leaf and gray growth underneath in humid weather. [Leaf spot](richfarm://pests-diseases/leaf_spot) produces brown or dark marks that expand. Remove affected leaves, water at soil level, and keep the canopy open. On a leafy crop, use a product only after identifying the cause and checking the label's harvest interval.
