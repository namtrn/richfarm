Lemongrass grows as a clump, has strongly scented leaves, and can be harvested repeatedly when the soil drains well. It handles sun but declines when its roots stay submerged, and prolonged cold can weaken the whole clump. Give it warmth, moderate moisture, and divide it when the center becomes crowded.

## Choose sun and the right regional season

Lemongrass grows strongly in full sun with enough room for the leaves to arch outward. In tropical Vietnam, it can be planted during warm weather, provided the soil drains after heavy showers. In a temperate part of the United States, it grows outdoors during the warm season; a container makes it possible to move the clump to a bright, sheltered place before cold weather.

Long leaves may fall across paths after rain or as the clump expands. Leave space around it so the foliage dries and does not rub against neighboring plants.

## Water enough while roots establish

New divisions need evenly moist soil until fresh leaves appear. After that, feel the surface before watering. When it starts to dry, soak the root area and let excess water drain fully. Rainy weather calls for less watering.

Do not leave water in the saucer or pooled in the center of the clump. Drooping leaves do not always mean drought; if the soil is still wet, check the roots and soil aeration before adding more water.

## Use draining soil and light organic feeding

Loose, organic-rich soil with good drainage lets the roots expand without staying airless. Mix mature compost or well-rotted organic matter into the bed before planting. If new leaves are slow and pale despite enough sun, spread a thin layer of compost around the edge of the canopy or use a diluted liquid fertilizer according to its label.

Keep fertilizer away from tender leaf bases and never feed dry soil. Too much nitrogen produces soft, crowded foliage and makes the crown harder to inspect. After feeding, watch new growth instead of increasing the dose immediately.

## Divide the clump to renew it

Division is the practical way to propagate lemongrass when a clump has many shoots and a crowded center. Lift the clump while the soil is workable, separate shoots with healthy bases and their own roots, and replant them at about the same depth. Trim some of the long leaves so a new division loses less water while it recovers.

Keep the soil moderately moist after replanting. If the roots were disturbed heavily, give the division lighter sun at first and move it gradually to a brighter position once new leaves appear. A rooted piece of the base can grow on, but a leaf segment without a crown will not replace the plant.

## Cut outer leaves and harvest usable stalks

When needed, cut outer leaves close to the base with a clean knife, leaving the tender center to keep growing. Remove dry or damaged leaves so the canopy stays open. For stalks, choose a thick shoot, loosen or lift the base, and cut what is needed without pulling up the entire clump.

After harvesting, clear leaves that lie against the soil and check for water pooled in the crown. A clump harvested in sections usually recovers more reliably than one stripped of all its leaves at once.

## Check early for pests and disease

[Aphids](richfarm://pests-diseases/aphids) may occasionally gather on new leaves, causing curling or sticky foliage. Inspect fresh growth and the leaf bases deep inside the clump; rinse a small infestation with water. Crowded growth and excess nitrogen can make inspection more difficult.

If the clump wilts while the soil is wet, or the lower bases become soft and the roots turn brown and mushy, suspect [root rot](richfarm://pests-diseases/root_rot). Remove damaged tissue, reduce watering, improve drainage, and clean tools before dividing another clump. Use a treatment product only after identifying the cause and reading its safety directions.
