## Light and location

Onions are sun-loving plants that need full sun to develop bulbs. A spot with direct sun for most of the day produces large, firm bulbs; too much shade gives plenty of leaves but small bulbs. They prefer mild weather while young and more sun once the bulb begins to swell.

This is a low plant with leaves growing in a clump from the base, so it needs no support. Container growing works if the pot is deep enough, but the plants need even spacing so the bulbs have room to swell; planting too close gives small bulbs.

## Watering

Keep the soil evenly moist while the leaves develop and the bulb begins to swell. Water when the top layer begins to dry; long dry spells give small bulbs, while soil kept soggy invites bulb rot.

As the leaves begin to yellow and flop near the end of the season, taper off watering. Dry soil at this stage lets the bulbs mature and store well; heavy watering late in the season makes the bulbs hold water and spoil quickly after harvest.

## Soil

Choose loose, fertile soil with good drainage. Growing on a raised bed or in loose soil helps the bulbs develop evenly and makes harvesting easier. Work well-rotted compost into the soil before planting; avoid fresh manure, which encourages bulb rot.

## Feeding

Onions need steady nutrition while the leaves grow, since healthy leaves are the foundation for a large bulb. Prepare the bed before planting, then feed lightly during leaf growth. Once the bulb begins to swell, cut back on nitrogen, since too much keeps the plant making leaves instead of bulb.

Watch the leaves before adding more. Pale leaves and slow growth may signal a nutrient shortage. Keep concentrated fertilizer away from the stem and do not feed while the soil is dry.

## Sowing and propagation

Onions can be grown from seed, from small sets, or from transplants, depending on the region and season. Seed takes longer and is usually sown early; sets are faster and easier for beginners. Choose a variety suited to the day length of your area, since some types only bulb well when the days are long enough.

When planting sets, set them shallowly with the tip poking above the soil and firm the soil around the base. Keep the soil moist until the roots establish and the leaves green up. In warm climates, plant in the cool season; in cold regions, sow seed or plant sets at the time suited to the local growing season.

## Ongoing care and harvest

Weed around the plants regularly, since onions compete poorly with weeds, especially when young. The upright leaves make watering and weeding easy; keep the soil loose around the base so the bulb develops evenly.

When the leaves turn yellow, flop over, and the tops begin to dry, the bulbs are mature. Stop watering and let the bulbs dry in the soil for a few days if the weather is dry, then lift the whole plant and cure the bulbs in an airy spot until the skins dry. Well-cured bulbs store for a long time. Harvest in time before heavy rain rots the bulbs.

## Common pests and diseases

[Thrips](richfarm://pests-diseases/thrips) are a common pest on onions, leaving silvery or whitish streaks on the leaves, curling them and slowing growth. Check the leaf axils and bases while watering; keeping the plant well watered and healthy helps it cope. [Aphids](richfarm://pests-diseases/aphids) may also gather on young leaves.

In damp conditions, [leaf spot](richfarm://pests-diseases/leaf_spot) can cause unusual spots on the leaves; remove affected leaves and avoid wetting the foliage. If the bulbs turn soft and rot, check the drainage and reduce watering; this may involve [root rot](richfarm://pests-diseases/root_rot), so identify the exact cause before treating anything.
