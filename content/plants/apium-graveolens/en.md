Celery is a cool-season biennial cultivated for its crisp, succulent, aromatic ribbed stalks and nutrient-rich leaves. Native to marshlands, it requires extraordinarily consistent soil moisture, ample rich organic nutrients, and mild growing temperatures. The non-negotiable rule in celery care is preventing moisture deficits, which immediately render the stalks tough, stringy, and bitterly pungent.

## Light and location

Celery thrives under 5-6 hours of gentle sunlight daily. It prefers cool temperatures between 15-21°C; severe heat waves harden stalk fibers, enhance bitterness, and trigger bolting.

Situate containers where they receive morning sun and sheltering afternoon shade, or under shade cloth in warmer transitional climates.

## Watering

Given its marshland ancestry, celery demands constant, unflagging moisture. Soak thoroughly daily so the soil never dries out even momentarily.

While demanding abundant water, containers must still drain freely at the base to prevent stagnant conditions that lead to crown rot.

## Soil and potting mix

Celery requires fertile, humus-heavy soil with superior moisture retention and a pH of 6.0-6.8. Blend quality garden loam with abundant compost, worm castings, and coco coir.

Use planters with at least 20-25 cm depth and bottom drainage holes lined with fine mesh to retain moist potting substrate.

## Fertilizer

Celery is a heavy consumer of nutrients over its lengthy growth cycle. Heavily enrich soil with well-rotted manure or compost prior to planting.

Feed every 2 weeks with diluted, balanced organic liquid fertilizers to sustain steady, tender stalk development.

## Planting and propagation

Celery seeds are minuscule and slow to germinate; soak in lukewarm water overnight and surface-sow lightly onto moist seed mix. Germination requires light and takes 10-15 days.

Transplant sturdy seedlings into garden beds or containers when they reach 7-10 cm tall, spacing plants 15-20 cm apart.

## Care and harvesting

Optional blanching: loosely wrap cardboard collars around the stalks 1-2 weeks before harvest to exclude sunlight, reducing strong bitterness and yielding tender, pale stems. Hand-weed carefully around shallow roots.

Harvest in 80-100 days by snapping or cutting individual outer stalks as needed, allowing the central rosette to produce fresh growth, or cut the entire plant at ground level.

## Common pests and diseases

Inspect stalk bases and leaf undersides for [aphids](richfarm://pests-diseases/aphids) and [thrips](richfarm://pests-diseases/thrips). Chewing [caterpillars](richfarm://pests-diseases/caterpillars) may target soft leaves.

Overly humid or crowded plantings invite fungal [leaf spot](richfarm://pests-diseases/leaf_spot) or soil-borne [root rot](richfarm://pests-diseases/root_rot). Ensure ample spacing and direct irrigation onto the soil rather than over the foliar crown.
