## Light and location

Snake plant is one of the most forgiving houseplants about light, growing steadily from dim corners to bright indirect light. It also tolerates gentle morning sun, but harsh direct sun all day can scorch the leaves, especially on varieties with yellow edging. Brighter light gives thicker leaves and clearer pattern.

This is a succulent with upright leaves growing in a clump from the base, so it needs no support. Offsets grow around the mother plant and can be separated when crowded. A pot with drainage holes is the most important thing; the plant tolerates being root-bound and dislikes frequent repotting.

## Watering

Snake plant is one of the most drought-tolerant houseplants, and overwatering is the most common cause of death. Water only when the soil has dried completely, checking a few centimeters deep by hand first. Soak it, then let the soil dry fully before watering again; in the cold season or in low light, it may need water only every few weeks.

Do not leave water pooled in the leaf rosette, since this causes rot. Wrinkled leaves may mean prolonged dryness, but a soft, mushy leaf base with a musty smell usually means too much water and root rot.

## Soil

Use a very well-draining mix made for succulents, or potting soil amended with coarse sand or perlite. The pot needs clear drainage holes and should never be left standing in water. Soil that holds water for long is the main cause of root rot.

## Feeding

The plant needs little fertilizer. A diluted liquid fertilizer applied as directed a few times during the growing season is enough; stop feeding in the cold season. Do not feed while the soil is dry. Since it grows slowly, it needs far less nutrition than most leafy houseplants.

## Sowing and propagation

The simplest way to propagate snake plant is to divide the offsets that grow around the mother plant. Gently lift an offset that has its own roots, pot it in well-draining mix, and keep it slightly moist until it roots. Leaf cuttings also work, a piece of leaf placed in the mix will root, but plants grown from leaf cuttings take much longer.

When repotting, choose a slightly larger pot with fresh mix. The plant tolerates being root-bound, so repot only when the roots fill the pot or the soil is depleted.

## Ongoing care and harvest

Wipe dust off the leaves regularly with a damp cloth to keep them photosynthesizing and looking clean. Cut away damaged or yellowing leaves near the base with a clean knife or scissors. Rotate the pot so the plant grows evenly on all sides if needed.

Yellow or mushy leaves usually come from overwatering; check the soil and cut back on watering before doing anything else. Wrinkled, dull leaves in a very dark spot may mean too little light; move the plant to a brighter place gradually.

## Common pests and diseases

[Mealybugs](richfarm://pests-diseases/mealybugs) can cluster at the leaf bases and in the leaf axils as white cottony spots; wipe them off with a cotton swab or damp cloth when you first notice them. Check the leaf bases regularly, since this is where they hide easily.

Overwatering causes [root rot](richfarm://pests-diseases/root_rot): the leaf base turns soft and mushy, yellow-brown, with a musty smell. If this happens, stop watering, lift the plant from the pot, cut away the damaged parts, and repot into fresh, well-draining mix. Do not water again until the soil has dried completely.
