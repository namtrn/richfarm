## Light and location

Oregano is a Mediterranean shrub that loves full sun and needs at least 6 hours of direct sunlight a day. Plenty of sun gives strongly flavored leaves and a compact shape; too little light makes the plant stretch and the leaves lose flavor. It handles dry soil and hot weather well.

The plant grows as a low bush that spreads wider over the years and does not climb, so it needs no support. It suits garden beds, containers, or gaps between paving stones; in a pot, choose a wide container, since the plant spreads sideways.

## Watering

Oregano tolerates drought well and cannot handle soil that holds water for long. Water when the top layer of soil has dried completely; let the soil dry fully between waterings. Overwatering or poor drainage weakens and eventually kills the plant.

Once established, its water needs drop noticeably; water less often rather than frequently. In containers, check the moisture by hand before watering; in the rainy season, make sure water drains quickly from the base.

## Soil

Choose well-drained, somewhat dry soil; the plant does well in sandy soil or mix amended with grit or perlite. Overly rich soil gives soft, less aromatic leaves. Avoid heavy soil that holds water; use a raised bed or container if the garden soil drains poorly.

## Feeding

The plant needs almost no fertilizer. A small amount of well-rotted compost worked in at planting is enough for most cases; overly rich soil makes the plant grow leggy and lose flavor. If growth is slow and the leaves look pale in poor soil, feed lightly with a diluted liquid fertilizer as directed.

## Sowing and propagation

Oregano can be grown from seed, cuttings, or division. The seed is small; sow it shallowly in well-draining soil and keep it lightly moist until germination. Cuttings are faster: take a healthy young shoot, place it in well-draining medium, and keep it lightly moist until roots form. Division is simplest once the plant has spread into a clump.

Plant at the start of the growing season so the roots establish in time. Young plants need more regular watering for the first few weeks, tapering off once they are established.

## Ongoing care and harvest

Light, regular trimming keeps the plant compact and produces fresh growth evenly. Avoid cutting deep into old wood, which sprouts poorly. A spreading plant can be left alone or trimmed as you prefer; trimming after flowering restores its shape.

Pick leaves and young stems whenever you need them. Fresh leaves are most aromatic when picked in the morning; cut young stems and strip the leaves, or use whole small sprigs. To dry them, cut the stems before the plant flowers and hang them in an airy place out of direct sun; dried leaves keep their flavor for a long time when stored airtight.

## Common pests and diseases

[Spider mites](richfarm://pests-diseases/spider_mites) can appear in hot, dry weather, pale-stippling the leaves and leaving fine webbing; check the leaf undersides and keep the plant healthy. [Aphids](richfarm://pests-diseases/aphids) may gather on young shoots; a light infestation can be washed off or removed by hand.

In prolonged damp conditions, [root rot](richfarm://pests-diseases/root_rot) from water-retentive soil can wilt and kill the plant; stop watering and check the drainage first, and repot into a better-draining mix if it is in a container. [Powdery mildew](richfarm://pests-diseases/powdery_mildew) can appear when the canopy is dense and damp; thin the plant and keep it open.
