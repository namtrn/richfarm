Sweet potato is a vigorous warm-season vining crop prized for its sweet, nutritious storage roots and edible tender shoot tips. It revels in full sunshine and handles heat with ease, but poorly drained soil quickly causes root rot. The primary cultivation goal is providing deep, loose soil and moderating nitrogen so energy flows into root expansion rather than excessive leaf mass.

## Light and location

Sweet potato vines require 6-8 hours of unfiltered direct sunlight daily. Shaded plantings produce lush, elongated vines with negligible root development.

Position in open garden plots, mounded ridges, or deep patio containers that enjoy maximum day-long sun exposure.

## Watering

While drought-tolerant once established, plants need consistent moisture during initial rooting and during the mid-season tuber-filling window. Water whenever the top soil layer dries out.

Withhold watering 2-3 weeks before harvesting to enhance sugars, firm up tuber skin, and reduce the risk of storage decay.

## Soil and potting mix

Optimal root development requires light, well-draining sandy loam with a loose root zone of at least 30-40 cm and a pH between 5.5-6.5. Compacted clay yields deformed, constricted roots.

For container culture, mix potting soil with rice hulls, perlite, and aged compost to guarantee friable texture.

## Fertilizer

Work generous amounts of well-composted organic matter into the beds prior to planting. Supplementary feeds should lean heavily toward potassium rather than nitrogen.

Excessive nitrogen triggers runaway vine expansion at the expense of root bulking. Supply potassium-rich organic amendments as tubers begin swelling underground.

## Planting and propagation

Propagate easily using vegetative vine slips 25-30 cm long with 5-6 leaf nodes. Insert slips at a shallow 30-45 degree angle, burying 2-3 nodes beneath the moist soil surface where tuberous roots form.

Alternatively, sprout slips from healthy root tubers placed in moist sand or shallow water.

## Care and harvesting

Lift wandering vines periodically to break adventitious rootlets forming along stem nodes, forcing the plant to concentrate its resources on the primary root cluster at the crown. Tender leaf tips can be harvested lightly for cooking without harming overall root yield.

Harvest storage roots 90-120 days after planting when the lower leaves begin to yellow. Dig carefully on a dry sunny day to avoid gouging the thin skins.

## Common pests and diseases

Tender leaves may host [aphids](richfarm://pests-diseases/aphids), [thrips](richfarm://pests-diseases/thrips), or chewing [caterpillars](richfarm://pests-diseases/caterpillars). Pick caterpillars by hand and maintain good weed sanitation around bed edges.

In saturated soils, fungal pathogens can induce [root rot](richfarm://pests-diseases/root_rot). Ensure high planting ridges and clean drainage conduits to keep root zones aerated.
