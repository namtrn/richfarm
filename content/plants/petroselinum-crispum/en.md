## Light and location

Parsley grows well in morning sun to light sun, about 6 hours a day. In hot climates it prefers afternoon shade over full blazing sun. It tolerates cool weather and is a perennial in mild regions, but is usually grown as an annual where summers or winters are extreme.

This is a low herb with leaves forming a clump from the base, so it needs no support. It grows well in containers or in the ground; leave space between plants so the canopy stays open.

## Watering

Keep the soil evenly moist; parsley has a fairly deep taproot but still needs steady water for soft, mild leaves. Water when the top layer begins to dry. Long dry spells make the leaves tough and slow growth, while soil kept soggy can rot the roots.

In containers the soil dries faster than in garden beds and needs checking more often. Water at the base and limit wetting the leaves to avoid leaf spot.

## Soil

Choose loose, fertile soil with good drainage. Work compost into the soil before planting to keep it soft and nourished. In containers, use a well-draining vegetable mix with clear drainage holes.

## Feeding

Because the plant keeps producing leaves, it benefits from steady but modest nutrition. If the soil was enriched with compost, a light feed with diluted liquid fertilizer after every few harvests is usually enough. Pale leaves and slow growth despite enough water may signal a need for more nutrients.

Keep concentrated fertilizer away from the stem and do not feed while the soil is dry. Too much nitrogen makes the leaves watery and less aromatic.

## Sowing and propagation

Parsley is usually grown from seed, but the seed germinates slowly and unevenly, sometimes taking weeks. Soaking the seed in warm water for about a day before sowing speeds germination. Sow shallowly on moist soil and keep the surface damp until the seedlings appear; patience is the key at this stage.

It can be sown directly where it will grow, or started in a tray and transplanted once the seedlings have a few true leaves. Stagger the sowings a few weeks apart to harvest leaves continuously.

## Ongoing care and harvest

Cutting the outer leaves regularly encourages new leaves from the center and keeps the plant compact. Cut whole outer leaves or stems near the base, leaving the center to keep growing; do not strip the whole plant at once. In the second year the plant flowers and the leaves may lose flavor; let it flower for seed or start new plants.

Pick leaves while young and deep green, ideally in the morning when they are freshest. Use clean scissors to cut near the base of the leaf you are taking. With regular picking, parsley keeps producing for months in suitable conditions.

## Common pests and diseases

[Aphids](richfarm://pests-diseases/aphids) gather on young shoots and leaf undersides, curling leaves and slowing growth. Check while watering; a light infestation can be washed off or removed by hand. [Snails and slugs](richfarm://pests-diseases/slugs_snails) may eat young leaves in damp spots, especially on seedlings.

In damp conditions, [leaf spot](richfarm://pests-diseases/leaf_spot) can cause unusual spots on the leaves; remove affected leaves, avoid wetting the foliage, and keep the planting area open. If the leaves take on a white powdery coating, this may be [powdery mildew](richfarm://pests-diseases/powdery_mildew); cut out the affected parts and watch the remaining plants. On a culinary herb, identify the exact cause before using any product.
