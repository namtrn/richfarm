Sweet corn is a warm-season cereal crop renowned for its plump, golden kernels bursting with natural sweetness. Because corn is wind-pollinated, it must always be planted in rectangular blocks rather than single isolated rows. Crucial cultivation imperatives include unobstructed full sunshine, fertile soil, and steady irrigation through the critical tasseling and silking windows.

## Light and location

Sweet corn demands 6-8 hours of direct sunshine daily to support thick stalk development and sugar synthesis in developing kernels. It grows vigorously in warm conditions between 20-30°C.

Always plant in grid-like blocks of at least 3-4 parallel rows to ensure wind carries pollen effectively from upper tassels down to lower ear silks.

## Watering

Water needs are significant and non-negotiable during two vital phases: when tassels emerge and when silks appear during kernel fill. Soak the root zone thoroughly in the morning.

Moisture stress during silking causes missing kernels, poorly filled ear tips, and diminished sugar levels.

## Soil and potting mix

Deep, fertile, well-draining loam or sandy loam with a pH of 6.0-6.8 provides the ideal foundation. The root zone must hold moisture while permitting rapid downward drainage.

Form raised beds or mounds 20-25 cm high to promote deep brace root anchorage and prevent water stagnation.

## Fertilizer

Sweet corn is a heavy feeder that responds eagerly to high fertility. Incorporate generous quantities of aged manure or compost into the planting beds.

Side-dress with nitrogen-rich organic amendments when plants reach 30-40 cm tall, and supply potassium inputs when tassels form to maximize kernel fullness.

## Planting and propagation

Always direct-sow corn seeds, as taproots resent root disturbance. Plant seeds 2-3 cm deep in warm, moist soil; seedlings emerge in 4-7 days.

Space plants 25-30 cm apart within rows, maintaining 60-70 cm between adjacent rows to facilitate air currents and root spread.

## Care and harvesting

Hill soil up around the lower stalk bases to encourage prop brace roots, fortifying stalks against wind lodging. Snap off small basal suckers to channel resources into the primary ears.

Harvest when silks turn brittle, dark brown, ear tips feel rounded and blunt, and a punctured kernel yields milky sweet sap. Pick in the cool early morning and prepare promptly before sugars convert to starch.

## Common pests and diseases

Watch for corn earworms and chewing [caterpillars](richfarm://pests-diseases/caterpillars) entering the silk channel. [Aphids](richfarm://pests-diseases/aphids) occasionally congregate along tassels and leaf sheaths.

Humid spells can trigger foliar [rust](richfarm://pests-diseases/rust) or fungal [leaf spot](richfarm://pests-diseases/leaf_spot). Waterlogged ground fosters [root rot](richfarm://pests-diseases/root_rot).
