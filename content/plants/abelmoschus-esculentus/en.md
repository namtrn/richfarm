Okra is an upright herbaceous plant that grows quickly in hot, sunny weather. It flowers and produces pods continuously for several weeks. Harvest the pods while they are young and tender; if they stay on the plant too long, they become fibrous, tough, and less pleasant to eat.

## Light and location

Okra prefers hot weather and produces best when grown in a spot with plenty of direct sunlight. Long periods of shade or low light can make the plant tall and weak and reduce flowering and pod production.

Because the plant has an upright stem and large leaves, it needs an open position where air can move freely between plants. Avoid locations exposed to repeated strong winds. If the site is windy, add a stake as the plant grows to help support the stem.

## Watering

Okra tolerates dry conditions fairly well, but the soil should stay evenly moist from flowering through early pod development so the pods remain tender and do not become fibrous. Water when the surface layer of soil begins to dry, and water deeply enough to reach the root zone. If the soil is still moist, wait before watering again.

Apply water directly around the base and avoid wetting the leaves when possible, which helps reduce disease on the foliage. After heavy rain, reduce watering and check drainage because okra does not tolerate standing water around its roots for long. A layer of dry straw or other mulch can help the soil hold moisture during hot weather.

## Soil

Choose loose, organic-rich soil with good drainage. Mix in mature compost or well-rotted manure before planting. If growing okra in a container, use a large pot with clear drainage holes and do not leave the base sitting in water after watering.

Soil that stays waterlogged can weaken the roots and cause yellow leaves or wilting even when the soil is wet. Avoid low spots or locations where water commonly collects after rain.

## Feeding

Incorporate well-rotted organic matter before planting to give the crop a steady nutrient base. Because okra can produce pods continuously for a long period, it benefits from regular feeding during the growing season.

Avoid applying too much nitrogen at once. Excess nitrogen can push leafy growth at the expense of pods and leave the stems soft and more likely to lean. Once flowering begins, apply a light side-dressing about every two weeks, using a low dose according to the product label and the fertility of the soil.

If the leaves are pale and the plant is growing slowly despite receiving enough water, it may need additional nutrients. Moisten the soil lightly before feeding and keep concentrated fertilizer away from the stem.

## Sowing and propagation

Okra is usually grown from seed. Sow directly into the soil once the weather and soil have become consistently warm. Soaking the seeds in water for half a day to a day before sowing can help them germinate more quickly and evenly. After sowing, keep the soil adequately moist; in warm soil, seeds often germinate in about 6 days.

Sow 2–3 seeds in each planting hole and cover them with a thin layer of soil. Once the seedlings have developed several true leaves, thin them so that only the strongest plant remains in each hole. Space plants about 35 cm apart.

## Ongoing care and harvest

The upright stems can grow fairly tall. In windy locations, use a simple stake to keep the plants from leaning when they are carrying pods. Keep weeds under control around the base and maintain an open growing area so the plants are easier to inspect and pests have fewer places to hide.

Harvest young pods early and regularly, usually every 2–3 days during the main season. Pods that remain on the plant too long become mature, fibrous, and tough, and can slow the production of new pods. Regular picking encourages the plant to keep flowering and extends the harvest period.

The first pods are often ready about 60 days after sowing, although the timing depends on growing conditions. Choose pods that are young, tender, and easy to snap. Cut the stem with clean scissors rather than pulling, taking care not to scrape the main stem or nearby developing pods.

## Common pests and diseases

[Slugs and snails](richfarm://pests-diseases/slugs_snails) may eat the leaves and stems of newly emerged seedlings, especially in damp, sheltered areas. Check around the base and underneath leaves in the early morning or evening, when they are most active. Keep the planting area open, remove fallen leaves, and avoid leaving objects close to the base where pests can hide.

[Leaf spot](richfarm://pests-diseases/leaf_spot) can produce unusual spots on the leaves and spread in persistently damp conditions. Remove affected leaves as soon as possible, clean cutting tools after use, and avoid watering the foliage late in the day. Do not plant too densely; good spacing helps the leaves dry after rain.

If the plant wilts while the soil is still wet, do not water again immediately. Check drainage around the base first, since roots damaged by overly wet soil can also cause the upper parts of the plant to wilt. If the problem continues or affects several plants, look for signs of [root rot](richfarm://pests-diseases/root_rot) before deciding how to treat it.
