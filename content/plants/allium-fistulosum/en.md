Scallion (bunching onion) is an indispensable culinary staple that produces upright, hollow tubular green leaves with crisp white succulent bases. It grows rapidly, thrives in compact container spaces year-round, and readily regrows after repeated cuttings. Primary care objectives center on consistent light moisture, porous drainage, and light base-mounding to elongate the sweet white stems.

## Light and location

Scallions thrive with at least 5-6 hours of daily sunlight to sustain sturdy green tubular foliage and concentrated aromatic oils. They perform well on sunny windowsills, balconies, or open raised garden beds.

Ensure adequate ventilation around container clusters to avoid stagnating humidity.

## Watering

Because scallions feature fibrous shallow root systems, consistent moderate moisture is essential. Irrigate gently in the early morning; add a light drink in the late afternoon if container soil dries out under warm breezes.

Prevent soggy saturation; stagnant water around leaf sheaths rapidly causes crown rot and foliar dieback.

## Soil and potting mix

Scallions require loose, humus-rich soil with prompt drainage and a pH between 6.0-7.0. Blend standard potting soil with compost, aged rice hulls, or perlite to preserve aeration.

Use planters or shallow troughs 15-20 cm deep equipped with ample base drainage apertures.

## Fertilizer

Enrich the planting medium with worm castings or well-rotted compost. Following each harvest cut, supply mild, diluted organic liquid feed to accelerate vigorous shoot regrowth.

Avoid applying concentrated fertilizers directly against the tender stems to prevent fertilizer burn.

## Planting and propagation

Propagate easily by division of established root clumps or by planting healthy rooted scallion bottoms. Trim foliage back to 3-5 cm above the root plate and set 2-3 cm deep in moist soil.

When growing from seed, sow shallowly at 0.5 cm depth; germination takes place in 7-10 days in evenly damp soil.

## Care and harvesting

Hill small amounts of friable soil or compost around the stem bases periodically to blanch and lengthen the crisp white shank. Hand-pull competing weed seedlings early.

Harvest in 30-45 days by cleanly snipping green stems 2-3 cm above the soil line to permit continuous regrowth, or harvest the entire clump roots and all.

## Common pests and diseases

[Thrips](richfarm://pests-diseases/thrips) are the primary insect challenge, rasping leaf surfaces and creating silver streaks and dry tips. Occasional [caterpillars](richfarm://pests-diseases/caterpillars) may chew through outer leaf walls.

In sustained humid weather, monitor for fungal [leaf spot](richfarm://pests-diseases/leaf_spot) or soil-borne [root rot](richfarm://pests-diseases/root_rot). Ensure free-draining conditions and thin crowded clumps.
