## Light and location

Peace lily is a shade-loving houseplant that grows well in medium to bright indirect light. It cannot handle harsh direct sun, which burns the leaves; in too-dark spots it flowers less and the leaves pale. Brighter light encourages more even flowering.

This is a herbaceous plant with leaves growing in a clump from the base, so it needs no support. It forms a full bush and can be divided when it gets crowded. Grow it in a pot with drainage holes; since the plant likes humidity, keep it in an airy spot away from dry drafts.

## Watering

Peace lily is a plant that signals clearly when it needs water: the leaves droop when the soil dries and perk back up soon after watering. Keep the soil evenly moist but not soggy; water when the top layer begins to dry or when the leaves start to droop slightly. Avoid letting the soil dry out for long, since the leaf tips will yellow and never fully recover.

In the cold season or in low light, the plant needs less water. Never leave the pot standing in water after watering. The plant enjoys humidity; misting lightly around it or setting the pot on a tray of damp pebbles keeps the leaves fresher, especially in dry air.

## Soil

Use a loose mix that holds moisture well while still draining. A houseplant mix amended with coco coir or perlite holds water evenly without waterlogging. The pot needs clear drainage holes.

## Feeding

The plant needs little fertilizer. A diluted liquid fertilizer applied as directed every few weeks during the growing season encourages flowering; reduce or stop in the cold season. Pale leaves and sparse flowers are more often a sign of too little light than too little fertilizer; check the position before increasing the dose. Do not feed while the soil is dry.

## Sowing and propagation

The most common way to propagate peace lily is by division. When the plant fills the pot, lift the whole clump, gently separate it into smaller clusters, each with its own roots, and pot them separately. Water well after planting and keep them in a sheltered spot out of harsh sun for the first few days.

Repot when the roots fill the pot, usually every 1-2 years; choose a slightly larger pot with fresh mix.

## Ongoing care and harvest

Wipe dust off the leaves regularly with a damp cloth so they photosynthesize well. Cut away yellow or damaged leaves near the base with clean scissors. The flower consists of a white spathe with a small spadix in the center; when the spathe turns green and fades, cut the whole flower stalk near the base so the plant channels energy into leaves and the next bloom.

Drooping leaves are not always from dry soil; they can also result from temperature shock, repotting, or overwatering that damaged the roots. Check the soil before watering more: if the soil is still wet and the leaves droop, inspect the roots and drainage.

## Common pests and diseases

[Mealybugs](richfarm://pests-diseases/mealybugs) cluster in the leaf axils and at the leaf bases as white cottony spots; wipe them off with a cotton swab or damp cloth when you first notice them. [Spider mites](richfarm://pests-diseases/spider_mites) appear when the air is dry, yellowing the leaves and leaving fine webbing; raising the humidity helps keep them in check.

Overwatering causes [root rot](richfarm://pests-diseases/root_rot): yellow leaves, a soft stem base, and a musty smell from the soil. If this happens, stop watering, inspect the roots, and repot into a better-draining mix if needed. If the leaves develop spreading spots, this may be [leaf spot](richfarm://pests-diseases/leaf_spot); remove the affected leaves and avoid wetting the foliage when watering.
