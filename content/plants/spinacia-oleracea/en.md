Spinach is a nutrient-dense, cool-weather leafy green prized for its tender texture and mild, earthy sweetness. It is highly prone to premature bolting when exposed to rising temperatures or long daylight hours. The top priority in spinach care is growing in cool conditions, maintaining steady soil moisture, and harvesting promptly before flower stalks form.

## Light and location

Spinach thrives under 4-6 hours of gentle sunlight daily. Growth is optimal at temperatures of 15-20°C; heat above 25°C triggers immediate bolting, rendering foliage bitter and leathery.

In transitional or warm seasons, provide partial afternoon shade to moderate root zone temperatures and prolong the harvest window.

## Watering

Because spinach has a shallow root system, consistent and moderate moisture is vital. Irrigate gently at the base early in the day to avoid splashing grit onto tender leaf folds.

Drought stress rapidly accelerates bolting, whereas standing water causes damp, suffocated roots that quickly rot.

## Soil and potting mix

Spinach requires fertile, humus-rich soil with superior drainage and a neutral to slightly alkaline pH between 6.5-7.0. It performs poorly in acidic soils (pH below 6.0), exhibiting stunted growth and yellowed foliage.

For containers, choose shallow troughs or planters 15-20 cm deep with unhindered drainage ports.

## Fertilizer

Amend soil with rich compost or worm castings prior to sowing. When seedlings establish 3-4 true leaves, apply light doses of dilute nitrogen-rich organic liquid feed to encourage broad, succulent leaves.

Discontinue supplemental feeding 7-10 days before harvest.

## Planting and propagation

Grow spinach directly from seed, as roots dislike transplant disruption. Soak seeds in lukewarm water for a few hours before sowing 1 cm deep; seeds germinate in 5-10 days in cool soil.

Thin young seedlings to 10-15 cm apart to ensure good ventilation and give individual rosettes room to expand.

## Care and harvesting

Hand-pull encroaching weeds carefully to avoid damaging shallow root systems. Lightly mulch around plants to cool the soil and keep leaves clean from soil splash.

Begin harvesting 35-45 days after sowing. Cut outer leaves individually to allow continuous production from the center, or harvest the entire rosette before flower spikes initiate.

## Common pests and diseases

Inspect leaf undersides for colonies of [aphids](richfarm://pests-diseases/aphids). [Slugs and snails](richfarm://pests-diseases/slugs_snails) and chewing [caterpillars](richfarm://pests-diseases/caterpillars) may target tender foliage overnight.

In damp, crowded microclimates, [downy mildew](richfarm://pests-diseases/downy_mildew) can produce yellow foliar lesions with gray fungal growth. Promote air circulation and avoid evening overhead irrigation to prevent [root rot](richfarm://pests-diseases/root_rot).
