Pothos can survive in low light, but it will grow slowly, produce paler leaves, and lose some of the contrast in its white or yellow variegation. Overwatering is more likely to cause yellow leaves and root rot than a little dryness. The main priorities are a stable bright spot, letting the potting mix dry partway between waterings, and trimming long vines so the plant stays full.

## Find a bright spot without harsh sun

Pothos grows steadily in medium to bright indirect light. Strong direct sun can scorch the leaves, especially on heavily variegated varieties. In a very dark corner, growth slows, the leaves fade, and the variegation becomes less distinct.

Choose a fairly stable position; if you need to move the plant, let it adjust gradually to the new light level. Pothos has aerial roots, so you can let the vines trail from a hanging basket or guide them up a moss pole or stake, depending on the space available.

## Let the soil dry partway before watering

Pothos tolerates dryness much better than waterlogging. Before watering, check a few centimeters below the surface with your finger. If that layer is dry, water slowly around the base until water runs from the drainage holes. Empty any water left in the saucer so the bottom of the pot does not stay submerged.

In cool weather or low light, the soil dries more slowly and the plant needs less water. If the leaves droop while the soil is still wet, do not water again right away; the roots may be short of air because the mix is staying wet too long. If the soil is dry well below the surface and the leaves are drooping and yellowing, the plant may be thirsty instead.

Large leaves collect dust easily. Wipe them gently with a damp cloth to help them receive light and to make early signs of trouble easier to spot on the undersides.

## Keep the potting mix light and the roots airy

Use a loose, fast-draining mix rather than heavy soil. A houseplant mix amended with coco coir, rice hulls, or perlite gives the roots more air and prevents water from lingering. The pot needs clear drainage holes; a layer of gravel at the bottom cannot replace them.

Pothos needs little fertilizer. If it is producing new leaves steadily, there is no need to feed it. When growth slows or new leaves look pale despite a suitable position and moisture level, spread a thin layer of well-rotted compost over the soil, keeping it away from the stem. If using liquid fertilizer, dilute it and apply a small amount to soil that is already slightly moist. When the dose is unclear, start small and watch the plant instead of increasing the concentration.

## Let the vines trail or climb, depending on the space

The vines can trail naturally from a hanging basket or climb a moss pole or stake in the pot. As the plant climbs, gently guide the aerial roots near the support and tie the vines loosely if they need help staying in place. Do not tighten the tie around the stem as it thickens.

If the plant is trailing, rotate the pot occasionally so the vines receive light more evenly. If you want a compact shape, cut back the longest vines instead of forcing too many stems into one crowded spot.

## Trim long vines to keep the plant full and make new plants

When a vine gets too long, cut just above a leaf node. The remaining stem can produce new shoots, while the cutting can become a new plant. Choose a healthy stem with 2-3 nodes, remove the lower leaves, and place it in water or moist potting mix.

If rooting in water, move the cutting into a pot once the roots are a few centimeters long. If rooting in soil, keep the mix slightly moist but never soggy and place the cutting in bright indirect light. Several cuttings can share one pot to fill it out faster.

When the plant becomes crowded or the roots have filled the pot, separate sections with their own roots or move the plant into a pot one size larger. Avoid moving it into a much larger pot, since the extra soil can stay wet for too long.

## Check the leaves, base, and soil to catch problems early

Check the growing tips, leaf axils, undersides of leaves, and soil around the base when growth slows or the leaves change color. These checks help distinguish low light, overwatering, and pest problems before they spread.

**Pests.** [Mealybugs](richfarm://pests-diseases/mealybugs) cluster in the leaf axils and along the stems as white cottony spots; wipe them off with a cloth or cotton swab when first seen. [Spider mites](richfarm://pests-diseases/spider_mites) are more likely in dry air, yellowing the leaves and leaving fine webbing; wiping the leaves and raising the humidity around the plant can help. [Fungus gnats](richfarm://pests-diseases/fungus_gnats) increase when the potting mix stays wet, so let the soil dry more between waterings.

**Root problems.** [Root rot](richfarm://pests-diseases/root_rot) often comes with yellow leaves, a soft base, and a bad smell from the pot. If you suspect it, stop watering, inspect the roots, and repot into a better-draining mix if needed. Do not water more simply because the leaves are drooping.
