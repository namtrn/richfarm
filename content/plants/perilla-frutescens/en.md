Perilla produces aromatic leaves, often green with purple-red coloring, and forms a fuller plant when its tips are harvested early. It declines when the soil stays dry for too long, yet constantly wet soil weakens the roots and encourages leaf spots. Give it gentle light, start a new batch before the old plant ages, and inspect the leaf undersides throughout the season.

## Choose a mild season and moderate light

Perilla grows well in warm to mild weather with morning sun and light afternoon shade during hot periods. In a temperate part of the United States, sow or transplant it outdoors after cold weather has passed; a late-summer batch can provide leaves in mild autumn weather. In Vietnam, the cooler season often produces thicker, better-colored leaves.

Keep the area around the plant open so foliage dries after rain. If stems stretch, weaken, and carry small leaves, check the light before adding fertilizer.

## Water by soil condition, not a fixed schedule

Feel the top layer before watering. When it begins to dry, water slowly around the base until the root area is moist, then let the surface drain. After rain, reduce watering and check that the drainage holes remain clear.

Do not spray the canopy late in the day. A thin mulch layer can slow drying, but leave a gap around the stem so the crown stays open. If leaves droop while the soil is wet, inspect the roots and check whether the mix is compacted before watering again.

## Use organic soil and feed a leaf crop lightly

Mix mature compost or well-rotted organic matter into the soil before sowing. The mix should be loose and moisture-retentive without holding water for long periods. If leaves pale or new tips slow after repeated harvests, spread a thin layer of compost around the canopy edge or use a diluted liquid feed according to its label.

Keep concentrated fertilizer away from the stem and never feed dry soil. Excess nitrogen makes soft, crowded foliage and can encourage pests. Watch leaf color and the number of new shoots before feeding again.

## Start a new batch before the plant ages

Perilla seed is small, so sow it shallowly on moist soil and cover it lightly. The tray surface should feel moist but not sloppy or covered with standing water that washes seed away or constricts seedling stems. Once seedlings have several true leaves, thin or move them to separate pots while keeping the root ball intact.

Start another batch before the older plant flowers heavily and produces fewer young leaves. To keep a container plant going, a rooted division can be moved early in the growing season.

## Pinch tips for more young leaves

Once a stem has several strong nodes, cut or pinch the tip just above a pair of leaves. Side shoots will develop there and make the plant wider. Continue picking tender tips rather than only removing old basal leaves; use clean scissors and leave enough foliage for recovery.

If the plant is grown for leaves, remove flower clusters as they appear. If seed is wanted, keep a few healthy clusters until they dry on the plant.

## Check early for pests and disease

[Aphids](richfarm://pests-diseases/aphids) gather on tips and leaf undersides, curling or coating the foliage. [Caterpillars](richfarm://pests-diseases/caterpillars) leave chew holes, while [slugs and snails](richfarm://pests-diseases/slugs_snails) may feed on young leaves overnight in damp areas. Inspect the canopy in the morning and wash or hand-pick a light infestation.

[Leaf spot](richfarm://pests-diseases/leaf_spot) creates brown or dark marks that spread across leaves. Remove affected leaves, keep water off the canopy, and thin crowded stems for airflow. If wet soil is followed by yellowing and weak growth, improve drainage to reduce the risk of [root rot](richfarm://pests-diseases/root_rot). For an edible herb, use a product only after identifying the problem and checking the label's harvest interval.
