## Light and location

Rosemary is a Mediterranean shrub that loves full sun and grows best with 6 or more hours of direct sunlight a day. Plenty of sun gives fragrant leaves and a compact shape. In too little light the plant stretches, thins out, and loses aroma.

It grows as a small woody bush and does not climb, so it needs no support. Give it room to develop into a long-lived shrub; in a container, choose a pot wide enough for the fairly strong root system. In cool, damp climates, grow it in a pot so it can be moved to a drier spot when needed.

## Watering

Rosemary tolerates drought well and tolerates waterlogging very poorly; soil that holds water for long is a common cause of death. Water when the top layer of soil has dried completely, soak it, then let the soil dry fully before watering again. In the rainy season, make sure water drains away quickly from the base.

Potted plants need attention, since the soil can both dry out and become waterlogged; check the moisture by hand before watering. Once the plant is established with good roots, its water needs drop noticeably; water less often rather than frequently.

## Soil

Choose soil with very good drainage that stays on the dry side. Sandy soil, or potting mix amended with grit or perlite, lets water drain quickly. Avoid heavy soil that holds water; if the garden soil is too heavy, a raised bed or container is safer.

## Feeding

Rosemary needs little fertilizer; overly rich soil gives soft, less aromatic growth that tolerates drought poorly. A light amount of well-rotted compost worked in at planting is enough for most cases. If growth is slow and the leaves look pale, feed lightly with a diluted liquid fertilizer as directed.

Keep concentrated fertilizer away from the stem and do not feed while the soil is dry.

## Sowing and propagation

Rosemary can be grown from seed, but germination is slow and uneven; stem cuttings are the common, faster method. Take a firm, healthy shoot about 10-15 cm long, strip the lower leaves, and place it in well-draining medium. Keep it lightly moist until roots form, avoiding a soggy mix.

Once the cutting is established and producing new leaves, move it to a pot or the final sunny spot. Plant at the start of the growing season so it can root in before harsh weather.

## Ongoing care and harvest

Light, regular trimming keeps the plant compact and produces fresh growth evenly. Avoid cutting deep into old wood, which sprouts poorly; cut above the part of the stem that still has green leaves. After flowering, trim lightly to restore the shape.

Pick leaves and young stems whenever you need them. Cut young stems and strip the leaves, or use whole small sprigs; fresh leaves are most aromatic when picked in the morning. To dry them, tie the stems in small bunches and hang them in an airy place out of direct sun.

## Common pests and diseases

[Spider mites](richfarm://pests-diseases/spider_mites) can appear in hot, dry weather, pale-stippling the leaves and leaving fine webbing; check the leaf undersides and keep the plant healthy. [Aphids](richfarm://pests-diseases/aphids) may gather on young shoots; a light infestation can be washed off or removed by hand.

In prolonged damp conditions, rosemary is prone to [root rot](richfarm://pests-diseases/root_rot) from water-retentive soil: leaves yellow and branches wilt even while the soil is wet. If this happens, stop watering and check the drainage first; in a pot, repot into a better-draining mix. [Powdery mildew](richfarm://pests-diseases/powdery_mildew) can appear when the canopy is dense and damp; thin the plant and keep it open.
