Chayote (chayote squash) is a vigorous perennial climbing vine celebrated for its mild, crisp fruits and delectable tender leafy shoot tips. It climbs aggressively, thrives best in temperate or mild highland climates, and requires robust, expansive trellising to support its heavy canopy. Cultivation priorities include building sturdy trellises, providing consistent root moisture, and protecting the planted fruit crown from rotting in saturated soil.

## Light and location

Chayote requires at least 6 hours of sunlight daily alongside fresh air movement. It thrives in moderate temperatures between 18-25°C; extreme summer heat above 32°C stunts vine extension and induces flower abortion.

Build trellises in open, high-ground garden sections or sunny rooftop spaces that catch natural breezes.

## Watering

With its vast foliar surface, chayote transpires moisture quickly and demands steady, deep irrigation. Water thoroughly in the morning, keeping root zones evenly moist especially during vine expansion and fruit filling.

Prevent waterlogging; the sprouting seed fruit and crown are vulnerable to fungal decay if standing water persists.

## Soil and potting mix

Soil must be deep, friable, humus-rich, and freely draining with a pH between 6.0-6.8. Dig generous planting pits 50-60 cm wide and incorporate liberal quantities of well-rotted manure, compost, and coarse matter.

Mound the planting collar 15-20 cm above surrounding grade to avoid standing rainwater accumulation.

## Fertilizer

Generously enrich planting pits with aged compost before planting. Once vines colonize the overhead structure, top-dress monthly with balanced organic fertilizers or vermicompost.

As flowering intensifies, supplement with organic potassium inputs to promote firm, heavy fruit set.

## Planting and propagation

Chayote is uniquely propagated by planting the entire mature, single-seeded fruit that has sprouted a shoot from its basal cleft. Place the sprouted fruit on its side in moist soil, burying roughly two-thirds of the fruit while leaving the emerging shoot tip exposed.

Erect a heavy-duty arbor or wire trellis 1.8-2 m overhead prior to rapid vine extension.

## Care and harvesting

Train emerging runners evenly across the trellis framework. Tender vine tips can be pinched regularly for culinary use while simultaneously encouraging bushier secondary fruiting stems.

Harvest fruits 30-35 days after flowering when they reach mature size with smooth, tender skin and soft prickles. Pick systematically with sharp shears to stimulate ongoing fruit production.

## Common pests and diseases

Watch for [aphids](richfarm://pests-diseases/aphids) and [thrips](richfarm://pests-diseases/thrips) on tender growing tips. [Slugs and snails](richfarm://pests-diseases/slugs_snails) frequently attack vulnerable newly sprouted shoots at ground level.

Prolonged damp overcast periods can invite [leaf spot](richfarm://pests-diseases/leaf_spot) or subterranean [root rot](richfarm://pests-diseases/root_rot). Prune dense lower foliage to optimize canopy air circulation.
