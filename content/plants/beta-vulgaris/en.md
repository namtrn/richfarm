Beetroot (red beet) is a versatile, cool-season dual-purpose crop prized for its sweet, earthy, deep-purple globes and nutritious tender salad greens. It develops rapidly, thrives in cool conditions, and requires steady moisture to yield round, tender roots free of woody internal zoning. Essential care practices include stone-free friable soil, timely seedling thinning, and consistent watering.

## Light and location

Beetroot thrives with 5-6 hours of unfiltered sunshine daily to power leafy crowns and concentrate sugars in expanding taproots. Optimal temperatures range between 15-22°C; extreme heat makes roots woody and pale.

Select open garden beds, raised mounds, or sunny container positions with uninterrupted morning light.

## Watering

Consistent moisture is essential for tender, succulent root flesh. Water deeply early in the morning; irregular drought followed by heavy soakings causes split rinds and tough concentric rings within the beet flesh.

Ensure drainage is unrestricted, as saturated soils suffocate root tips and encourage fungal decay.

## Soil and potting mix

Roots demand light, friable, stone-free loam rich in organic matter with a neutral to slightly alkaline pH of 6.5-7.5. Beetroot suffers in acidic soils (pH below 6.0), exhibiting stunted roots and leaf margin scorch.

For containers, mix compost and perlite into potting soil to preserve loose, uncompacted root volume.

## Fertilizer

Amend beds with well-cured compost or worm castings prior to sowing. Once roots begin expanding, supply light potassium and phosphorus inputs.

Avoid excess nitrogen fertilizers, which stimulate immense leaf tops while yielding diminutive, pale roots.

## Planting and propagation

Direct-sow seeds, as taproots dislike disturbance. Each beet 'seed' is actually a cluster containing multiple embryos; soak in lukewarm water for 3-4 hours before sowing 1-2 cm deep.

Thin emerging seedlings when 5 cm tall, leaving 8-10 cm between plants so swelling globes have room to expand freely. Thinned tops make excellent salad greens.

## Care and harvesting

Hand-weed beds carefully around swelling crowns. Lightly mulch around exposed root shoulders to conserve moisture and maintain cool soil conditions.

Harvest in 50-65 days when roots measure 4-6 cm across. Avoid leaving roots in ground until oversized, as they rapidly become tough and fibrous.

## Common pests and diseases

Monitor foliage for [aphids](richfarm://pests-diseases/aphids) and [thrips](richfarm://pests-diseases/thrips). Chewing [caterpillars](richfarm://pests-diseases/caterpillars) may consume leaf blades.

In persistently humid weather, foliage can succumb to Cercospora [leaf spot](richfarm://pests-diseases/leaf_spot). Poorly drained substrates invite damp-rot and [root rot](richfarm://pests-diseases/root_rot).
