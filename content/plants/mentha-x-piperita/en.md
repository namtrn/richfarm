## Light and location

Mint grows well in gentle to bright sun and survives in light shade. More sun produces thicker, more aromatic leaves, but in hot climates a little afternoon shade keeps the plant from wilting. It tolerates a wide range of temperatures and is a perennial in many regions.

The most important point about placement: mint spreads very quickly through underground stems and can take over a whole bed in one season. Grow it in a pot, or in a container sunk into the ground, to keep it in bounds. If planting directly in a bed, install a buried barrier or accept that the plant will roam outward.

## Watering

Mint likes evenly moist soil and handles moisture better than many herbs, but it cannot tolerate long waterlogging. Water when the top layer begins to dry; the plant wilts quickly in dry soil, especially in a small pot.

In pots, the soil dries faster than in garden beds on hot days. On the other hand, if the soil in the pot holds water too long, the roots can become waterlogged and rot. Check the drainage holes and never leave the pot standing in water after watering.

## Soil

Choose loose, fertile soil that holds moisture well. Mint is not fussy about soil, but rich, humusy soil produces steady, fragrant leaves. In containers, use a well-draining vegetable mix; a larger pot keeps the soil moisture more stable.

## Feeding

Mint needs little fertilizer. If the soil was enriched with compost, the plant usually has enough nutrition; feed lightly with a diluted liquid fertilizer only when the leaves look pale or growth slows. Keep concentrated fertilizer away from the stem and do not feed while the soil is dry.

## Sowing and propagation

Mint is rarely grown from seed; the usual methods are stem cuttings or division. Cut a healthy piece of stem with several nodes and place it in water or moist growing medium; roots often appear within 1-2 weeks. Once the plant forms a dense clump, lift a cluster of rooted underground stems and plant them in a new pot or spot.

Because it spreads quickly, check regularly that the underground stems have not escaped the pot. If you grow several pots, keep them apart so mint from one pot does not creep into another.

## Ongoing care and harvest

Mint keeps producing leaves when cut back regularly. Frequent tip cutting encourages branching and stops the plant from growing old and leggy. When the plant begins to flower, pinch off the flower buds to keep the leaves flavorful; unless you want seed, do not let it bloom heavily.

Leaves and young shoots can be picked year-round in suitable climates. Use clean scissors to cut above a pair of leaves, leaving healthy stem for regrowth. Cut around the clump evenly rather than stripping one side, so the plant keeps a balanced shape.

## Common pests and diseases

[Spider mites](richfarm://pests-diseases/spider_mites) can appear in hot, dry weather, causing pale, stippled leaves and fine webbing underneath. Keep the soil evenly moist and check leaf undersides regularly; wash them off early with a gentle spray. [Aphids](richfarm://pests-diseases/aphids) may also gather on young shoots.

In damp, poorly ventilated conditions, mint can develop [rust](richfarm://pests-diseases/rust), showing orange rust-colored spots on the leaves. Remove affected leaves, open up the plant, and avoid wetting the foliage. If the plant wilts while the soil is still wet, check the drainage first, since waterlogged roots can also weaken the plant; if the problem persists, look for signs of [root rot](richfarm://pests-diseases/root_rot) before treating anything.
