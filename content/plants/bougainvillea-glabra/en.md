Bougainvillea colors the bracts surrounding its small true flowers most heavily with strong sun and a moderate dry interval between waterings. Constantly wet soil produces leaves at the expense of colorful bracts, while thorny branches need safe support and pruning. Choose a permanent site, keep the base draining, and prune after a flowering flush.

## Give it strong sun and a mild dry period

Bougainvillea needs full sun for sturdy branches and colorful bracts. In tropical Vietnam, it can grow through the year; a moderate dry interval often supports flowering, but the plant should not be left wilted for long periods. In a temperate part of the United States, grow it outdoors where prolonged cold is uncommon or use a container that can move to a bright shelter before frost.

Choose a trellis, fence, or strong stake for climbing branches, but keep it away from a narrow walkway because the thorns can scratch. Dense, shaded growth usually produces green leaves rather than many bracts; check the site and light before adding fertilizer.

## Water when the soil has dried slightly

Soak the root area when the surface has dried, then let the soil drain and wait for it to dry slightly again. Container plants lose water faster on hot days; after rain, skip watering while the soil remains wet. A short dry interval can support flowering, but prolonged wilting means the plant is under stress.

Water at the base rather than wetting the canopy late in the day. Keep drainage holes open and never leave the pot standing in water. A thin mulch layer steadies the root zone, but leave a gap around the stem.

## Use light soil and little nitrogen

Loose, fast-draining soil with moderate organic matter supports strong flowering shoots. Mix compost at planting, then add only a thin layer when growth genuinely slows or leaves pale. With a packaged fertilizer, choose a modest amount according to the label and apply it when the soil is lightly moist.

Excess nitrogen produces lush leaves but fewer flowers. Keep fertilizer away from the trunk and avoid tearing roots while cultivating around the base. A container may need a light feed after pruning, but do not follow a rigid schedule while leaves remain green and branches are vigorous.

## Plant and root cuttings carefully

Set a transplant at the same soil level it occupied before and keep the root ball intact. For cuttings, take a firm healthy stem, make a clean cut, and place it in a draining medium kept moderately moist. Keep the cutting in gentle light while roots form, then increase the light gradually.

Wear gloves and use long-handled tools when working around thorns. Do not overwater a cutting to speed rooting, since saturated medium can rot the base before roots develop.

## Prune after flowers and train the branches

After a flowering flush fades, remove dead, inward-growing branches and shoots that have gone beyond the desired support. Light tip pruning encourages side shoots, but do not cut the canopy back hard just before a period of weak light. Use soft ties to train branches without constricting growing stems.

Spent bracts can be cut with their stalks to keep the plant tidy. If growth is all leaves with few colors, reduce nitrogen, increase light, and check whether the soil stays wet too long.

## Check early for pests and disease

[Aphids](richfarm://pests-diseases/aphids) may cluster on tips, [mealybugs](richfarm://pests-diseases/mealybugs) form white masses in branch joints, and [spider mites](richfarm://pests-diseases/spider_mites) cause yellow stippling and fine webbing in hot, dry weather. Inspect young shoots and leaf undersides; wipe or rinse small colonies.

[Powdery mildew](richfarm://pests-diseases/powdery_mildew) creates a powdery coating on leaves, while [root rot](richfarm://pests-diseases/root_rot) causes wilting even though soil is moist. Remove affected tissue, open the canopy, reduce watering, and improve drainage. Use safe tools around thorny branches and apply a product only after identifying the cause.
